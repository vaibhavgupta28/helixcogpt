## Summary
Privacy regulations and performance expectations are converging, forcing operators to adopt architectures that encode consent while preserving optimization headroom.

## What’s Changed
- Jurisdictions now require explicit consent lineage for all activation events.
- Data clean rooms are standard, but interoperability still requires tailored adapters and QA.
- Publishers and marketers both demand transparency, increasing the value of audit-ready telemetry.

## Architecture Patterns
- Model consent states as first-class objects and propagate them through enrichment pipelines.
- Apply differential privacy to aggregated metrics while preserving granularity for optimization models.
- Automate data retention and deletion workflows with policy-as-code enforcement.

## Risks & Mitigations
- **Risk:** Latency introduced by compliance checks. **Mitigation:** Parallelize validation and pre-cache approvals.
- **Risk:** Consent drift across partners. **Mitigation:** Run reconciliation jobs with immutable ledger storage.
- **Risk:** Measurement blind spots after anonymization. **Mitigation:** Blend modeled conversions with incrementality testing.

## Key Takeaways
Teams that embed privacy controls into core data engineering stacks sustain performance while remaining audit-ready across regions.
